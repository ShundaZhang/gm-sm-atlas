A P-256 field reduction routine cannot simply be renamed SM2: the moduli have different sparse forms. A generic big-integer ECC engine may support both after supplying the right complete domain and validation rules. Identical 32-byte coordinate widths do not make public points, private keys or certificates interchangeable.

02 / FIELD NOTES

Compare the equations, not just the curve names

NIST curves specify domains; ECDSA and ECDH specify operations.

AspectSM2 signatureECDSA
Message representativee=SM3(ZA||M)Hash of M, converted/truncated under the scheme rules
First componentr=(e+x([k]G)) mod nr=x([k]G) mod n
Second components=(1+d)⁻¹(k−rd) mod ns=k⁻¹(e+rd) mod n

Identity bindingExplicit ZA inputNot an intrinsic ECDSA input; protocol may bind identity separately

A raw ECDH core computes a shared point from one private scalar and a peer public point, followed by protocol-defined validation and derivation. SM2’s named exchange combines static and ephemeral keys plus ZA/ZB and confirmation. No mathematical curve substitution converts one complete scheme into the other.

03 / FIELD NOTES

Compare security level and representation separately

P-384 and P-521 are not 256-bit alternatives.

DomainField bits / coordinate bytesApproximate classical level
curveSM2256 / 32About 128 bits under generic ECC assumptions
NIST P-256256 / 32128
NIST P-384384 / 48192
NIST P-521521 / 66256

P-521 uses 66 bytes per coordinate and is not a “512-bit curve.” NIST SP 800-186 also covers other curve forms, including Edwards curves used by EdDSA. Edwards/Montgomery encodings and EdDSA operations are not drop-in SM2 or ECDSA replacements. secp256k1 is another 256-bit curve, but it is not NIST P-256.

These comparisons are classical and do not confer standard approval. Large fault-tolerant quantum computation would threaten the discrete-log assumptions behind both SM2 and NIST ECC; migration is a protocol and key-lifecycle problem, not a change of curve branding.
