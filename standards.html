Locate the exact standard and layer

GM/T, GB/T and an RFC do not mean the same thing.

ReferenceWhat it covers

GB/T 32905-2016 / 32907-2016SM3 / SM4 algorithm cores

GM/T 0001 / 0002 / 0003 / 0004ZUC / SM4 / SM2 / SM3 industry algorithm families

GB/T 35275; GM/T 0010SM2 message representations and structures

GB/T and GM/T use recommended-standard designations. Whether a particular system must use a standard depends on its actual requirements; a learning atlas does not make that determination. Read the issuer’s version/status record rather than inferring legal obligations from an algorithm name.

02 / FIELD NOTES

Distinguish current, future and historical material

Review date: 1 October 2026.

The official SM2 list shows GB/T 35275-2026, published 30 April 2026, with an implementation date of 1 November 2026. As of this review it is a forthcoming message-format standard, not an already-effective replacement. It does not by itself change the SM2 curve or signing equations. GM/T 0009-2023 is a different, updated industry usage document.

The English SM2, SM3 and SM4 Internet-Drafts linked here are expired explanatory drafts, not published RFC algorithm standards. They are useful for formulas and historical vectors but must be cross-checked with GB/GM texts. RFC 8998 is a published Informational RFC on an Independent Submission stream, with a narrow TLS 1.3 scope.

03 / FIELD NOTES

Keep three kinds of evidence

A correct output, interoperable bytes and assurance scope answer different questions.

Use a normative standard to identify required behavior; an implementor’s code and test vectors to inspect execution; a protocol profile to identify the wire representation and identity context. For product assurance, locate evidence tied to that exact module/build and scope. A NIST comparison is an engineering comparison, not recognition of GM algorithms as FIPS-approved algorithms.
